Jonathan Edwards, A Divine and Supernatural Light (1734)
Excerpt, spelling modernized
📖 Before You Read

Edwards preached this sermon at Northampton, Massachusetts, several years before the Great Awakening's peak, laying out the theological idea that would drive much of the revival's emotional intensity: true religious understanding is not merely intellectual.

"There is a twofold understanding, or knowledge, of good that God has made the mind of man capable of. The one is that which is merely speculative or notional... The other is that which consists in the sense of the heart... There is a difference between having a rational judgment that honey is sweet, and having a sense of its sweetness...

This spiritual and divine light... does not consist in any impression made upon the imagination... but it consists in a true sense of the divine excellency of the things revealed in the word of God... A person may have a rational belief that God is holy, and may argue it, and may be convinced of it by argument, without having any such sense of it as this... but this light gives a direct view of it, as a sense of its sweetness and beauty."
