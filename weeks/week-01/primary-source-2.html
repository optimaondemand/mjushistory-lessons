📖 Before You Read

Hariot joined the 1585 Roanoke expedition as a scientific observer, sent to catalog what the colony's backers might realistically hope to profit from and to record what he learned of the Algonquian-speaking peoples who lived there. He published this report in 1588 partly to answer, in his own words, "slanderous and shameful speeches" that had discouraged further investment in the colony.

"Because there have been diverse and variable reports, with some slanderous and shameful speeches bruited abroad by many that returned from [Virginia]... I have therefore thought it good, being one that have been in the discovery of this land... to impart so much unto you of the fruits of our labours as that you may know how injuriously the enterprise is slandered.

And that ye may the better conceive hereof, I have divided this treatise into three special parts. In the first I will make declaration of such commodities there already found or to be raised, which will be beneficial for us to trade withal. In the second I will set down all the commodities which we know the country by our experience doth yield of itself for victual and sustenance of man's life... In the last part I will make mention generally of the natural inhabitants, their natures and manners...

There is [an herb] which is sowed apart by itself and is called by the inhabitants uppowoc... The Spaniards generally call it tobacco. This uppowoc is of so precious estimation amongst them, that they think their gods are marvellously delighted therewith... We ourselves, during the time we were there, used to suck it after their manner, as also since our return, and have found many rare and wonderful experiments of the virtues thereof...

Most things they saw with us, as mathematical instruments, sea compasses, the virtue of the loadstone in drawing iron, a perspective glass whereby was showed many strange sights, burning glasses, wildfire works, guns, books, writing and reading... was so strange unto them, and so far exceeded their capacities to comprehend the reason and means how they should be made and done, that they thought they were rather the works of gods than of men... They could not tell whether to think us gods or men, and many times and in every town where we came, they would offer us peace... some also were persuaded that we could... cure all diseases only by touching them with things belonging to us."
