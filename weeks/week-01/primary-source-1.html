Letter of Columbus, Announcing His Discovery (1493)
Excerpt, translated and lightly modernized for classroom use
📖 Before You Read

Columbus wrote this letter aboard ship on his return voyage, addressed to Luis de Santángel, a Spanish court official who had helped arrange his funding. Copies were quickly printed and circulated across Europe; this is, in effect, the first press release in the history of the Atlantic world. Read it as a document written to persuade its audience that the voyage had been worth the investment.

"Sir, since I know you will have pleasure at the great victory which Our Lord hath given me in my voyage, I write this to you, from which you will learn how in thirty-three days I passed from the Canary Islands to the Indies, with the fleet which the most illustrious King and Queen, our sovereigns, gave to me, where I found very many islands filled with people without number, and of them all I have taken possession for their Highnesses, by proclamation made and with the royal standard unfurled, and no opposition was offered to me.

To the first island which I found I gave the name San Salvador, in remembrance of the Divine Majesty, who has marvellously bestowed all this... The people of this island, and of all the other islands which I have found and seen, or not seen, all go naked, men and women, as their mothers bore them... They have no iron, nor weapons, nor are they capable of using them, not because they are not well built and of handsome stature, but because they are very timorous...

This is not because anyone has done them harm; on the contrary, at every place where I have been, and have been able to have speech, I have given them of all that I had, such as cloth and many other things, without receiving anything for it, but they are naturally timid and fearful... They are so guileless and so generous with what they have, that no one would believe it who has not seen it...

In this island... there is gold in great quantity... I have not found, nor had any information of monsters, except an island which is here... which is inhabited by people who are regarded in all the islands as very ferocious, who eat human flesh. Finally, to speak only of what has been accomplished on this voyage... Our Lord in his goodness guiding me, I could not fail to have their Highnesses gain, along with these other things, a great many good and profitable subjects, converts to our holy faith."
