📖 Before You Read

Winthrop led the much larger Puritan migration to Massachusetts Bay beginning in 1630, a decade after Plymouth's founding. In his journal, he reflects on the covenant principle he believed bound any successful colony together.

"We are entered into covenant with Him for this work. We must be knit together, in this work, as one man. We must entertain each other in brotherly affection. We must be willing to abridge ourselves of our superfluities for the supply of others' necessities... We must delight in each other, make others' conditions our own, rejoice together, mourn together, labor and suffer together, always having before our eyes our commission and community in the work, as members of the same body.

For this end, we must be knit together as a city upon a hill, the eyes of all people are upon us... I have before remembered the Plymouth men, who wanted for years the comforts we now enjoy, and yet, being knit together by their own agreement before ever they set foot ashore, endured what they endured and built what they built, not as strangers thrown together by chance, but as a body covenanted to a single purpose."
